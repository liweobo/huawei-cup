- **Target / estimation:** Q1 models RVR rather than MOR. Q2 classifies one-minute video segments, but does not unambiguously name the AMOS target field. Q3 claims physical MOR from dark-channel transmission and road geometry.
- **Forecast target / origin:** Q4 explicitly forecasts after the last screenshot at 2016-04-14 07:39:11 (pp33–36). This is the clearest stated end-sequence origin in the set, although its step arithmetic is inconsistent.
- **Availability / sequence:** All 100 derived values are history. No future weather is used. Seq2seq recursively feeds its prediction into the next step (p36), so observed future-target teacher forcing is not established for the published future curve.
- **Strategy:** B FULL_SEQUENCE_EXTRAPOLATION. ARIMA is a model extrapolation; Seq2seq+Attention is RECURSIVE after a 10-input/2-output setup.
- **Validation / metric:** Historical augmented data before 07:39:11 are split 70/20/10, but split ordering and origin construction are not reported. Validation/test RMSE 6.16/10.32 concern short historical sequences and do not validate the long crossing extrapolation. Final Q4 crossing validation is NO_VALIDATION.

## Q1

Pages 5–11 combine both dates, model RVR against same-time pressure, temperature, humidity and wind, and choose nonlinear transforms on the same 11,520 observations. R²=0.8018 is an in-sample fit statistic. Classification: TRAIN_FIT_ONLY and contemporaneous estimation. The 3,000m RVR plateau is treated as an ordinary piecewise level rather than a censored measurement process.

## Q2

Pages 11–18 split the 11-hour video into one-minute, 1,500-frame clips and average four visibility records per minute. Six classes are used: 50–100, 100–200, 200–500, 500–1,000, 1,000–3,000 and >3,000m; no ≤50m class is listed. A ResNet34+LSTM model uses a 60/20/20 dataset split and reports 17.23% error on 138 test videos, concentrated in low-visibility samples.

The paper does not state a complete watermark-to-LOCALDATE/UTC rule, tolerance or drift handling, and it does not clearly identify whether the labels are MOR or RVR: TEMPORAL_ALIGNMENT_UNVERIFIED / TARGET_FIELD_UNSPECIFIED. Split ordering/grouping is UNKNOWN, so the test result cannot be promoted to temporal or event generalization.

## Q3 scale

Pages 18–26 use dark-channel transmission, guided filtering, Koschmieder’s relation and perspective road geometry. The paper cites 6m lane stripes, 9m gaps and camera installation height 8–12m, while model assumption 3 fixes camera height at 10m. These are EXTERNAL_SOURCE plus AUTHOR_ASSUMPTION and remain REFERENCE_ONLY_INPUT. The black-target/0.05 contrast convention is assumed rather than scene-measured.

The reported series rises from about 30m to 60–68m. A conditional physical mapping exists, but its scene calibration, mapping of averaged transmission to depth, and highway MOR error are unverified. Output: ABSOLUTE_MOR_METRES; identifiability: PARTIALLY_SUPPORTED.

## Q4 and static inconsistencies

ARIMA(2,2,6) is fit to the full trend component; Seq2seq training data are created by random 3–5 second interpolation between the 100 observations. No multiple historical issuance points test the 50-minute-plus extrapolation. The step is calculated as `(t100−t1)/100=41.25s`, although 100 observations contain 99 intervals. More seriously, crossing index 172.4 (or 160.9) is multiplied by the full step and then added to the **last-frame** time; the already observed first 100 indices are therefore counted again. This is a static origin/index conversion inconsistency, not a recomputed solution.

Appendix pp45–50 uses unrelated `2018-10-1` to `2019-1-30` dates and `trueOrders` variables for the ARIMA code, so it does not reproduce the stated 2020E series as printed. Numerical comparison to the frozen run is NOT_DIRECTLY_COMPARABLE. Evidence supports G2/G3/G4/G5, not G1.
