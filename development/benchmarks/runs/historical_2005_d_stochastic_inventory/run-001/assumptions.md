# Assumptions and claim limits

1. **Horizon / objective.** Q1-Q4 specify repeated ordering and no finite terminal date. MODELING_ASSUMPTION: minimize long-run expected loss cost per calendar day. A raw total over an unspecified horizon is not identifiable. Renewal reward uses E[cycle cost]/E[cycle days], not E[cost/days]. Q5 has a separately declared finite 120-day scenario, with no deletion of early days.
2. **Shortage units.** Primary c4 is yuan per lost item, consistent with Q1's “单位商品的损失”. The “/天” in Q2 and Q3 is a source conflict, not silently erased. Alternative: interpret the printed units literally as a charge on cumulative foregone-demand exposure until replenishment, p*r*shortage_duration²/2. This is an explicit alternative penalty functional, not backorders or a proved interpretation of the source. Report both; if the policy changes materially, no unconditional unique numerical answer exists.
3. **Supply protocol.** There is at most one outstanding order. Arrival quantity is target minus residual stock at receipt, as needed to implement the stated receipt-time top-up; the source does not specify an order-time fixed quantity. Lead draws do not depend on ordered quantity or policy. No purchase cost or maximum order quantity is invented.
4. **Storage.** Own inventory is min(I,Q0), rented inventory (I-Q0)+; overflow is held in rented space at c3, never clipped/discarded. Goods use own capacity before rented capacity, with cost-free transfers as stock declines. Joint allocations are reserved static volumes a_i<=b_i, sum a_i=Q0, sum b_i=Q; no cross-item transfers are modeled. These operating details are assumptions.
5. **Lost sales.** Demand when stock is zero is recorded as lost and never fulfilled later. Holding inventory is nonnegative. No backorder state is present. The alternative penalty still does not deliver historical lost demand.
6. **Fluid / timing.** Constant deterministic rates deplete inventory continuously. Fractional items and continuous volume allocations are allowed as a fluid approximation; Q2 integer thresholds are also reported. An order follows threshold crossing; receipt occurs after X days. Between events, demand/holding are integrated exactly; at a shared timestamp receipt precedes policy review. No arbitrary daily clipping or arrival/demand batching.
7. **Initialization / regeneration.** Each Q1-Q4 replication starts immediately after replenishment at the fixed target; each subsequent receipt returns to this same state with no pending order. Cycle leads are iid conditional on the selected law. Hence cycles are regenerative, though time-point inventory observations are serially dependent. No warm-up is needed for renewal expectations. Replication ratios have finite-N ratio bias; N/2N checks assess it.
8. **Distribution estimation.** Q2 uses the empirical PMF as a minimally parametric estimator; short consecutive observations do not prove iid/stationarity. Split-half and moving-block resampling sensitivity are retained. Q4's continuous uniform interpretation is checked against discrete uniform {1,2,3}.
9. **Q5 observations and policy.** The scenario assumes actual customer requests, including unmet requests, can be recorded. Without this, sales are censored and the simple adaptive estimator is not justified. Daily request multipliers are fully specified SCENARIO inputs; a 14-day trailing mean uses only past observed requests. Arrival targets and own allocations stay fixed; only reorder threshold adapts. Replenishment at/after terminal time is not credited; pending orders and lost demand are reported. No claim of optimal adaptation or empirical validation is made.

These assumptions are part of each result's scope. Changing them requires a distinct scenario/record, not retroactive relabeling as source facts.
