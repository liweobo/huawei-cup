# Validation Report

The primary evidence for Q1-Q4 is a closed-form regenerative renewal-reward computation, so the simulator is a cross-check rather than the source of the headline numbers. Q5 is a declared finite-horizon scenario with simulation as its primary evidence. Every simulation estimate is reported with a mean, standard error and 95% interval over independent complete trajectories, never over individual periods.

Random-number provenance is fixed and recorded in `results/environment.json`: NumPy 2.2-style `Generator(PCG64)` with a `SeedSequence` derived from master seed 20260920. Independent streams are seeded per experiment namespace and replication, and policy comparisons bind the same scenario draws across policies so the paired differences are computed on identical lead-time realizations.

Small-case and exactness checks (`validation/model-checks.json`). Two hand-computed single-cycle costs match exactly (expected 33, actual 33; expected 57, actual 57). The zero-lead, long-lead and exact-enumeration checks give a maximum error of `0.0` under both the lost-sale and the exposure penalty. The finite three-day deterministic oracle, including a terminal pending receipt, gives cost/day 11 as computed by hand. Hard-infeasible policies are rejected rather than penalized, so no infeasible policy is reported as a solution.

Analytic versus simulation agreement. Across all products and policies, the simulated cost/day converges to the analytic value as replications and path length increase. The residual ratio bias moves from roughly `-0.006` at 128 replications to roughly `-0.0003` at 256 replications, and the analytic value sits inside the simulated interval in every reported pair. For example, the joint primary policy reports 8.0501 with CI [8.0447, 8.0555] against an analytic 8.0483.

Conservation and feasibility (`results/single.json`, `results/joint.json`, `results/q5.json`, `validation/final-model-audit.json`). Per-interval conservation holds to at most `7.1e-15` for Q1-Q4 and `3.6e-13` for Q5. Capacity and nonnegativity violations are zero in every reported run, and every cycle contains exactly one order and one arrival. The total cost reconstructs from its components with a maximum component error of `1.8e-15`.

Serial dependence. A long inventory path is serially dependent, so uncertainty is computed from independent replications rather than from the number of periods. As an additional check, `validation/dependent-lead-sensitivity.json` reruns product 3 under circular-block lead dependence with block length 4 at 256 replications: cost 11.3586 with CI [11.3321, 11.3852], overlapping the iid and analytic values. Product 3's observed lag-1 autocorrelation near 0.47 is why this check was run.

Horizon and replication convergence. Path length was run at 512 and 1024 cycles and replications at 128 and 256. The reported decision variables and cost levels are stable, and the standard errors shrink as replications double. No steady-state claim rests on runtime alone; the long-run target for Q1-Q4 follows from the regenerative structure.

Distribution alternatives. The Q4 continuous-uniform reading was checked against the discrete-uniform `{1,2,3}` alternative. The discrete case pushes the service probability onto a numerical support boundary, which is recorded as `ESTIMATE_UNSTABLE`; its tiny positive stockout value is not treated as a reliable service probability. The capacity boundary check in `validation/boundary-sensitivity.json` shows that product 3's unconstrained optimum is a cost infimum approached as L rises to Q, not an attained interior optimum; the admissible integer optimum is L = 39, and the report warns against rounding L to Q while preserving the same stockout probability.

Protocol guard audit. `validation/ordered-protocol-probe.json` exercises the frozen Skill's protocol-change guard directly. A change in event execution order that is causally significant is reported as no change, with no validator error, because the normalization sorts list elements. The primary implementation's own event order was separately verified to be correct, so this is an audit-layer defect rather than a wrong result in this run.

Decisive limitations. The Q2 lead samples are short and their iid/stationarity status cannot be proven. The source's c4 unit conflict has no unique resolution, so the item-loss and exposure readings are both retained. Q5 is entirely scenario-driven. Under these limits the reported policy comparisons are model results within a declared scope, not unconditional real-world optima.
